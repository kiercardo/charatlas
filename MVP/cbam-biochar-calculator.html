Methodology & formula

CBAM cost per tonne of product = SEE × chargeable share × EUA price, where SEE is the specific embedded (direct) emissions and the chargeable share follows the official phase-in: 2.5% (2026), 5%, 10%, 22.5%, 48.5%, 61%, 73.5%, 86%, 100% (2034) — the mirror of the EU ETS free-allocation phase-out (CBAM factor 97.5% → 0%). This structure reproduces published worked examples exactly (e.g. 10,000 t × 1.9 tCO₂e/t × 2.5% × €70 = €33,250 for 2026; €72.75/t for SEE 2.0 at 48.5% and €75).

Biochar effect: substituting biochar for fossil reductant lowers SEE by replaced tonnage × (3.10 − biochar intensity) tCO₂e/t. The CBAM saving is the cost difference between baseline and reduced SEE. The break-even biochar price = fossil reductant price + (saving ÷ tonnes of reductant replaced) — the most you can pay per tonne of biochar and stay cost-neutral on CBAM alone.

What this deliberately excludes: green-steel price premiums (+5–20% reported in early offtakes), carbon prices already paid in the origin country (deductible under Art. 9), and default-value mark-ups (10–30% if verified data is missing). All three shift the economics further in biochar's favour or against defaults.

Before relying on these numbers: biochar is not an offset against CBAM — it works only by reducing actual embedded emissions. Biogenic zero-rating carries sustainability and documentation conditions that must hold for your specific supply chain. The fossil-reductant emission factor (3.10) and reductant use are engineering defaults, not audited figures. Free-allocation benchmarks under the SEFA methodology (IR 2025/2620) are product- and route-specific; this tool uses the standard simplification (chargeable share × SEE) that published cost models use. For declarations, use verified installation data and professional advice.
